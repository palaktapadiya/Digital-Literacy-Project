Stay Safe Online — Prevention Checklist
1. Never share personal information online
Do not share your full name, phone number, home address, college ID number, date of birth, or any government ID details on any public platform or with people you have only met online. Cybercriminals and bullies can use this information to target, locate, or impersonate you.
2. Set all social media profiles to private
Go into the settings of every platform you use — Instagram, Snapchat, Facebook, Twitter — and set your profile to private. Regularly review who can see your posts, stories, and personal details. Only accept follow or friend requests from people you personally know and trust.
3. Think before you post anything
Before posting any photo, comment, status, or story, ask yourself: would I be comfortable if my parents, professors, or a future employer saw this? Once something is posted online it can be screenshotted and shared instantly — even after you delete it. Your digital footprint is permanent.
4. Block and report bullies immediately
If anyone is sending you threatening, offensive, or harassing messages online, do not engage with them, do not retaliate, and do not try to reason with them. Block them immediately and report their account directly to the platform. Most platforms like Instagram and WhatsApp have clear and easy reporting options.
5. Save evidence before blocking
Before blocking a cyberbully, take clear screenshots of all abusive messages, comments, posts, or threatening content. Note the date, time, and platform. This evidence is critical if you decide to report the incident to your college authorities or to the police.
6. Be careful about what you like and share
Even passively liking, sharing, or tagging someone in an offensive post makes you a participant in cyberbullying. Always think about the impact your online actions have on real people. If you see someone being bullied online, report the content and support the victim instead of staying silent.
7. UPI Safety — Never share your UPI PIN with anyone
Your UPI PIN is equivalent to your ATM PIN — it is the final authorisation for any payment. No bank representative, UPI app, government official, or customer support agent will ever ask you for your UPI PIN. If anyone asks for it, it is a scam — disconnect immediately.
8. UPI Safety — Always verify before making any payment
Before confirming any UPI payment, carefully verify the recipient's name, UPI ID, and phone number. Scammers frequently create UPI IDs that look almost identical to legitimate ones, changing just one letter or number. If something feels even slightly wrong, cancel the transaction and verify through an official channel.
9. Use strong and unique passwords for every account
Never use the same password across multiple platforms. Use a combination of uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and special characters. Enable two-factor authentication on every platform that supports it — this adds a critical second layer of security even if your password is compromised.
10. Do not click on unknown or suspicious links
Whether received via SMS, WhatsApp, email, or social media — never click on links from unknown senders. These links may be phishing attempts designed to steal your login credentials, install malware on your device, or gain access to your UPI or banking apps.
11. Speak up and seek help
If you or someone you know is experiencing cyberbullying or any form of online harassment, do not suffer in silence. Talk to a trusted friend, family member, or college counsellor. You are not alone and help is available.
12. Report cybercrime officially
Report any cybercrime — including cyberbullying, online fraud, identity theft, or UPI scams — immediately at the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal: cybercrime.gov.in or call the National Cyber Crime Helpline: 1930 (available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, across India).
